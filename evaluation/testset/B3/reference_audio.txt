Today we are looking at how inflation is measured. Most of the time, inflation means a rise in a broad price index that represents the overall price level in the economy. Examples of broad indices are the consumer price index, the personal consumption expenditures price index and the GDP deflator. The inflation rate is usually worked out as the percentage change in the consumer price index over a year. Economists also look at core inflation, which leaves out food and energy prices, because those rise and fall more than other prices in the short term. Narrower indices, such as the producer price index, track prices in particular parts of the economy.
