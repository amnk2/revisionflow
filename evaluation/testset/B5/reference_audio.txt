Now let's compare covalent and ionic bonding. In a covalent bond, two atoms share a pair of electrons, and the balance of attraction and repulsion between them holds the atoms together. In an ionic bond, electrons are transferred instead, and the resulting ions are held together by electrostatic attraction. A single covalent bond shares one pair of electrons, a double bond shares two pairs, and a triple bond shares three pairs. When the two atoms have different electronegativities, the electrons are shared unequally, and we call that a polar covalent bond. Water is a good example, because oxygen pulls the shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen does.
