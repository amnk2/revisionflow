Let's look at the light-dependent reactions. A molecule of chlorophyll absorbs a photon and loses an electron. That electron is passed along an electron transport chain, which ultimately reduces NADP to NADPH. At the same time, a proton gradient builds up across the chloroplast membrane, and the enzyme ATP synthase uses this gradient to make ATP. The chlorophyll gets its lost electron back when a water molecule is split, in a process called photolysis, and this is what releases oxygen. Not every wavelength of light is useful. In green plants, chlorophyll absorbs mostly violet-blue and red light, and the green light that is not absorbed is why leaves look green.
