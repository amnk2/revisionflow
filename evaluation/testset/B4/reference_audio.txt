In this section we are going to talk about steam power. In the early years of the Industrial Revolution, most industrial power actually came from water and wind. The first commercially successful use of steam was a pump patented by Thomas Savery in 1698. Then, before 1712, Thomas Newcomen introduced the first successful piston steam engine, which was used to drain deep mines. Newcomen engines were very inefficient, but where coal was cheap they let mines go much deeper. The big change came with James Watt, working with his business partner Matthew Boulton. Watt added a separate condenser, which made the engine far more efficient. By 1800 Britain had an estimated ten thousand horsepower supplied by steam.
